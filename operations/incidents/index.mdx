Use these runbooks only with the released deployment inventory, dashboards, access paths, and change procedures. Safety, evidence integrity, tenant isolation, cleanup, report reads, and audit preservation take priority over quickly restoring all writes.

<Warning>
  No production deployment, on-call roster, alert routing, or dated exercise
  evidence exists. Every runbook is **Planned** and must be rehearsed before
  production use.
</Warning>

## Common command structure

1. acknowledge the alert and open one incident record with UTC timeline;
2. assign incident commander, technical lead, communications lead, scribe, and security/privacy/legal roles as needed;
3. verify environment, tenant scope, release/config/region, affected dependency, and whether evidence/side effects are trustworthy;
4. freeze deploys, migrations, key/config changes, and risky retries that could expand impact;
5. apply the runbook's narrow containment with actor/reason/time and preserve cleanup/report/revocation access when safe;
6. recover through reviewed artifacts/procedures, reconciling unknown side effects before retry;
7. pass explicit technical, security/privacy, tenant, evidence, and user-journey verification before restoring traffic;
8. communicate confirmed impact and next update without secrets, personal data, private targets, or unsupported assurances;
9. close temporary access/holds/overrides, capture measured outcomes, and create owned corrective actions.

Severity is based on confirmed/potential customer, security/privacy, financial/pubnet, integrity, availability, and blast-radius impact. Anyone may escalate; only the incident commander lowers severity with recorded evidence.

## Exercise record

Each rehearsal records runbook/version, date, environment/release/config, scenario/fault injection, participants/roles, detection/acknowledgement/containment/recovery/verification times, objective result, communications sent, access/override cleanup, gaps/manual steps, evidence links/digests, and corrective owners/dates. "Tabletop only" is distinct from a technical recovery exercise.

The release gate requires a dated passing record for every page and no undocumented critical step. Until then, the corresponding production capability remains blocked or explicitly risk-accepted by authorized governance.
