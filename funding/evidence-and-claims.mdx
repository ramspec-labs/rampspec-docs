RampSpec funding material must distinguish current program facts, project plans, verified implementation, measured outcomes, and award decisions. A technically plausible plan is not deployment evidence, and an application is not an award.

<Warning>
  RampSpec has no recorded SCF invitation, submission, eligibility decision, or
  award. It has no public testnet deployment, pilot evidence, or measured
  on-chain outcome. Funding and track claims therefore remain planned or
  unavailable.
</Warning>

## Track decision process

No track is selected yet. The decision owner must rerun this process immediately before submission:

1. Confirm the current Build tracks, round or invitation process, eligibility rules, jurisdiction constraints, and submission deadline from official sources.
2. Confirm the product's actual state: public releases, traction, users, on-chain footprint, testnet and pubnet evidence, team capacity, and delivery timeline.
3. Evaluate Integration only if the selected building block remains on the current Integration List and RampSpec can evidence the track's traction, integration-budget, and on-chain outcome requirements. Anchor Platform is listed at the refresh date; listing alone does not establish project eligibility.
4. Evaluate RFP only against a current published RFP whose required outcome actually matches RampSpec. General developer-tooling language is insufficient.
5. Evaluate Open only from the current official criteria and any invitation instructions; do not use it as a fallback without confirming fit.
6. Record the selected track, rejected alternatives, sources, evidence, reviewer, and decision date in an approved governance record.

Until that record exists, all material says `track decision pending`.

## Evidence package

A submission-ready package links immutable or publicly verifiable evidence for:

- a public testnet environment and deployment manifest;
- one complete SEP-6 or SEP-24 journey;
- one complete SEP-31 plus SEP-38 journey;
- an Anchor Platform integration report naming its version and configuration boundary;
- adversarial SEP-10 and SEP-45 authentication results;
- two authorized pilot letters or public integration statements;
- repository releases and contributor activity, with collection dates;
- Drips participation and merged work, without implying rewards or endorsement;
- measured defects found and time saved, with methodology and limitations;
- the security model, independent review scope, findings, and remediation state;
- a maintenance, hosting, incident, and ownership plan;
- milestone deliverables and costs tied to acceptance evidence.

Repository stars, forks, contributor counts, and issues are supporting signals only. They do not prove users, adoption, interoperability, impact, or delivery readiness.

## Anchor Platform evidence

An Anchor Platform claim names the exact image or release, configuration digest, enabled SEP modules, business-server responsibilities, fixture source, environment, target, timestamps, report ID, and reproducible commands. It distinguishes Platform behavior from RampSpec behavior and links failures to redacted evidence. A local Docker start is not a completed integration; a testnet journey is not pubnet production use.

## Pilots and metrics

Pilot evidence requires written permission, scope, environment, dates, participants or anonymization method, baseline, measurement method, raw-record custodian, exclusions, and a reproducible result. Report defects found as deduplicated confirmed findings, with disputed and unresolved findings separate. Report time saved against a named baseline and sample size. Never generalize one pilot to ecosystem adoption.

## Claim rules

Every public claim appears in the [claim register](/examples/funding/claim-register.planned.json) with a status, evidence URL, owner, verification date, refresh date, and approved wording. Valid statuses are `planned`, `implemented`, `locally-checked`, `testnet-verified`, `pubnet-verified`, `externally-reviewed`, `authorized-pilot`, and `awarded`. Higher status is never inferred from a lower one.

Do not claim:

- guaranteed SCF eligibility, invitation, track acceptance, selection, or award;
- SDF certification, partnership, endorsement, audit, or regulatory approval;
- legal or compliance approval from software tests;
- production or mainnet readiness from local, mocked, or testnet evidence;
- partner adoption, traction, or pilot outcomes without written permission and evidence;
- complete protocol coverage while required rules are skipped, blocked, or indeterminate;
- security readiness before the stated independent review and remediation are complete.

An award claim additionally requires an official decision record, amount/currency wording that matches the notice, permitted attribution, required disclaimer, and a current review of award terms. Remove or downgrade a claim when its evidence expires, becomes unavailable, or no longer supports the wording.
